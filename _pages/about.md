I am a PhD student in Geography at Oklahoma State University, where I work as a Graduate Research Assistant on remote sensing and GIS applications in agriculture. My dissertation investigates how climate and management practices shape agroecosystems in the **Southern Great Plains**, combining satellite and uncrewed aerial systems (UAS) imagery with in-situ observations.

Most of my current work focuses on **corn yield prediction**. I collect multispectral, RGB, and thermal imagery over irrigated field experiments, extract spectral and thermal features, and fuse them with weather data in machine learning models. The harder and more interesting question is generalization: a model that fits one season and one field is easy, but a model that holds up across seasons, management regimes, and climatic variability is what actually helps someone make a decision. That question drives my current manuscript.

Before coming to Oklahoma, I completed my M.Sc. and B.Sc. in Geography and Environment at Jahangirnagar University in Dhaka, Bangladesh, where I worked on nighttime light remote sensing, land use and land cover change, and coastal hazard assessment. I am an FAA-certified remote pilot (Part 107).

I am interested in how geospatial machine learning and Earth observation foundation models can extend field-level agronomic insight beyond the plots where the data was collected. If you work on similar problems, or are interested in collaborating, please get in touch.
